# 03 - Career Profile: Cover Letter Generation

This file governs COMPASS career-profile cover letter artifacts.

Generate a cover letter only when the user explicitly requests one.

A cover letter must be derived from the user's current instruction, verified Intake claim ledger and do-not-claim list when available, latest approved canonical career record, target job description for terminology and context only, current COMPASS analysis when available, and user-provided constraints.

Imported resumes, master CVs, LinkedIn profiles, prior cover letters, and similar artifacts may be used as evidence and provenance only unless their material claims have been verified through Intake.

Do not invent technologies, ownership, metrics, credentials, role history, company knowledge, personal motivation, domain experience, culture fit, mission affinity, or emotional connection.

Use a calm, professional, forward-looking tone.

Cover letters must be specific, source-grounded, non-generic, and additive to the resume. A cover letter should tell the reader something decision-relevant that the resume and job description do not already communicate clearly.

## Narrative Objective

A cover letter should normally develop one central argument about why the candidate's verified experience, judgment, operating model, or grounded connection to the opportunity merits continued conversation.

Before drafting, classify the relationship context when known:

- cold application;
- prior recruiter contact;
- completed recruiter screen;
- referral;
- direct hiring-manager interaction;
- established professional relationship.

Use established interaction context when it materially improves continuity. Do not make an existing professional conversation read like a cold application, and do not repeat role facts already established merely to demonstrate comprehension.

Before drafting, identify:

1. what the resume already proves;
2. what useful information the cover letter can add;
3. the letter's single central argument;
4. the strongest source-backed evidence or anchor story for that argument;
5. the narrative archetype that best fits the relationship context and available evidence.

## Adaptive Narrative Archetypes

Select one primary archetype. A secondary influence is allowed when natural, but do not splice multiple templates together mechanically.

### Conversation Continuation

Use when a prior recruiter, referral, or hiring-manager interaction contains a meaningful point worth carrying forward.

Typical progression:

> shared context -> what stuck with the candidate -> relevant evidence -> broader credibility -> natural continuation

### Story / Lesson Led

Use when one verified episode strongly demonstrates the target problem, judgment, or operating behavior.

Typical progression:

> brief story -> decision or lesson -> target relevance -> broader credibility -> close

### Operating-Model Fit

Use when the target role materially depends on how a senior individual contributor, architect, lead, or manager works across decisions, implementation, and stakeholders.

Typical progression:

> how the candidate works -> source-backed proof -> relevant example -> target connection -> close

### Problem / Insight Led

Use when the candidate has a genuine, supportable observation about the work that adds information rather than paraphrasing the employer's own description.

Typical progression:

> specific observation -> candidate approach -> evidence -> why it matters here -> close

### Direct Fit

Use when no stronger grounded personalization or narrative context is available.

Typical progression:

> concise positioning -> strongest evidence -> distinctive value -> close

Do not force personalization or an archetype whose required context is absent.

## Evidence and Technical Density

Use at least one source-backed value example when available.

Prefer one anchor story plus compressed supporting breadth over a catalog of projects, qualifications, or technologies.

Introduce implementation detail progressively:

> context or relevance -> decision/story -> selected technical proof

Technical specificity should make a decision, constraint, contribution, or consequence credible. It should not substitute for narrative purpose or require the reader to decode an implementation inventory before understanding why the example matters.

When the target role materially values a professional-effectiveness capability governed by `rules/20-professional-effectiveness-evidence.md`, prefer one or two concrete source-backed actions, decisions, tradeoffs, stakeholder interactions, or consequences that demonstrate the capability. Do not substitute generic labels such as `critical thinker`, `strong communicator`, `strategic`, `collaborative`, or `resilient` for evidence.

Professional-effectiveness evidence does not authorize stronger ownership, management, outcome, or scope language than the governing candidate source permits.

## Grounded Personalization

Personalization is valid only when grounded in established context. Valid sources may include:

- an actual recruiter or hiring-manager interaction;
- a specific aspect of the work the candidate has genuinely identified as interesting;
- a verified experience that shaped how the candidate approaches a problem;
- a source-backed working preference or operating pattern;
- real domain familiarity;
- a concrete, supportable observation about the opportunity.

Do not invent company-specific enthusiasm, personal motivation, culture fit, values alignment, mission affinity, role-specific affinity, or emotional connection.

Avoid generic filler such as "excited to apply" unless it is natural in context and adds useful meaning rather than substituting for source-backed fit.

## Portable Content Budget

Use the most specific known content constraint in this order:

1. an explicit application-provider or application-field character or word limit;
2. a current user-specific Source-of-Truth content limit;
3. the COMPASS default portable ceiling of **2,000 characters including spaces and punctuation**.

The applicable limit is a ceiling, not a quota. COMPASS has no generic minimum cover-letter length.

When the provider counts all pasted text, count every character that will be pasted, including greeting and signoff when present. DOCX-only candidate headers, contact blocks, dates, or presentation metadata do not consume the narrative budget unless they are part of the text that will be submitted into the constrained field.

When no application-field limit is known and a downloadable cover letter is produced, keep the canonical narrative within the same 2,000-character portable ceiling by default so the content remains reusable across application surfaces. A direct user instruction or current user-specific policy may set another limit.

## Semantic Review

Before release, review the finished letter for:

- **Resume recap:** Does the letter mostly translate resume bullets or employment chronology into prose?
- **Job-description echo:** Does the letter mostly restate the employer's own problem or requirements without adding candidate-specific insight?
- **Grounded personalization:** Can each personal-interest or connection statement be supported by known context, user instruction, verified experience, or an approved working preference?
- **Technical-density timing:** Does the reader understand why an example matters before encountering dense implementation detail?
- **Central argument:** Can the letter's main reason for continued conversation be expressed in one sentence?
- **Natural continuity:** When prior interaction exists, does the letter continue that relationship rather than unnecessarily resetting it?
- **Deletion test:** Can any sentence be removed without losing useful information, voice, narrative movement, or necessary social function?

These are semantic review requirements, not invitations to implement brittle phrase blacklists or regex-based "human" scoring.

A cover letter must not include internal COMPASS analysis, ATS tables, compensation strategy, objection notes, or private tactical notes.

## Structural Shell

Use the applicable user-specific presentation policy when one exists. At the generic framework level, preserve only this semantic-neutral order:

1. date or candidate presentation required by the active user policy, when applicable;
2. greeting;
3. narrative body;
4. closing;
5. signature.

The narrative body should not be forced into fixed semantic paragraph roles such as opening-fit, evidence, and role-alignment paragraphs.

If recipient details, company details, or role-specific motivation are not verified or provided, omit or generalize that part instead of inventing it.
